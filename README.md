## Overview

The Orang Bati ('men with wings' in Malay/Indonesian) are reported from the mountainous interior of Seram Island, the second-largest island in the Maluku archipelago of eastern Indonesia. According to indigenous Alfur community traditions, the creatures inhabit a network of caves on the slopes of Mount Kairatu (also called Gunung Binaiya, 3,027 m), emerging nocturnally to raid isolated mountain villages and abduct children. Physical descriptions are consistent: approximately five feet tall, with black leathery bat-like wings, red or reddish skin, and a long thin tail. Witnesses report hearing a long, mournful wail before attacks. The tradition appears to predate both Islamic and Christian missionary contact, as Christian missionaries from the 15th and 16th centuries recorded hearing accounts of a winged monster terrorizing the village of Uraur. In 1987, British missionary Tyson Hughes arrived on Seram for an eighteen-month contract and encountered these beliefs directly; he documented local accounts and claims to have personally witnessed a creature. Scientific investigation by television host Richard Terry concluded the creature was most likely the large flying fox (Pteropus vampyrus), which can reach wingspans of up to 1.5 meters and emits piercing screams. No physical specimen, photograph, or verifiable physical evidence has been documented. The Orang Bati remains unverified.

- **Origin**: From Malay/Indonesian orang (person, people) and bati (winged; from a root meaning to fly or have wings). The name is used by indigenous communities of Seram Island and was adopted in broader Indonesian usage.

- **First use**: Pre-contact Alfur oral tradition of Seram Island; documented in writing by Western sources in the 15th–16th century missionary accounts

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Large flying fox (Pteropus vampyrus)** _(Richard Terry (television investigator))_

The Orang Bati sightings are explained by encounters with the large flying fox (Pteropus vampyrus), a fruit bat native to Southeast Asia and Indonesia with wingspans up to 1.5 meters. Flying foxes are colonial, vocal, and visually impressive in flight — capable of causing genuine alarm in communities unfamiliar with their scale.

### Skeptical

**Cultural protective narrative** _(Comparative folklorists)_

The Orang Bati tradition functions as a cultural mechanism for enforcing community safety behaviors — particularly keeping children indoors at night when genuine dangers (predatory birds, disease, misadventure) are elevated. The creature narrative encodes practical safety rules in a memorable and emotionally compelling form.
